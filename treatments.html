Treatment options depend on an accurate diagnosis

Explore common categories of care and the questions to ask your clinician. Treatment suitability varies from person to person.

Clinical principle: No treatment can be recommended safely from website content alone. A clinician should explain your diagnosis, available options, expected benefits, limitations, risks and alternatives.

1. Assessment and diagnosis

A clinician may ask about symptoms, bowel habits, medical history, medicines and previous treatment. Depending on the presentation, examination or further tests may be recommended. The purpose is to identify the cause and rule out other conditions that can produce similar symptoms.

2. Conservative and non-surgical management

For some conditions, management may include changes to fibre and fluid intake where appropriate, avoiding excessive straining, activity or toileting advice, prescribed medicines, topical treatment or other measures tailored to the diagnosis. Do not start, stop or change medication based only on general website information.

3. Procedures and surgery

When a procedure is indicated, the clinician should explain what it involves, why it is being considered, anaesthesia arrangements, expected recovery, possible complications, alternatives and the chance that additional treatment may be needed. Options differ by condition and individual circumstances.

Questions to ask about laser procedures

  • Is this technique suitable for my specific diagnosis and disease severity?
  • What evidence supports its use in my case?
  • What are the alternatives, including non-surgical management?
  • What are the risks, recurrence possibilities, expected pain and recovery needs?
  • What is included in the quoted cost and follow-up plan?

4. Recovery and follow-up

Ask for written instructions about wound care, hygiene, bowel movements, diet, activity, medicines, follow-up and warning signs. Recovery varies by procedure and individual health factors. Contact your care team if you are unsure about a symptom after treatment.

5. Insurance and financial questions

Before a planned procedure, confirm the estimated cost, what is included, payment terms, insurer or TPA requirements, pre-authorisation and reimbursement documentation directly with the hospital and insurer. Coverage depends on your policy and approvals.

Safety note: Severe pain, heavy bleeding, fainting, fever with a painful swelling, or rapidly worsening symptoms should not wait for a routine appointment.

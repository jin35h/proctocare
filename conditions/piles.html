Piles (Haemorrhoids): Symptoms, Assessment and Treatment Questions

Piles, also called haemorrhoids, are swollen vascular cushions in and around the anal canal. Symptoms such as bleeding or discomfort can have other causes, so an appropriate assessment is important.

Important: This guide is general education and cannot diagnose your symptoms. A clinician should assess your individual situation.

What symptoms may occur?

Symptoms can include bright-red bleeding during bowel movements, itching, irritation, swelling, discomfort or a lump near the anus. Symptoms vary, and some people have no symptoms.

What can contribute to piles?

Straining, constipation, prolonged sitting on the toilet, pregnancy and other factors may contribute. A clinician can help assess relevant causes and individual risk factors.

How are piles assessed?

A clinician may ask about symptoms and medical history and may examine the area. Further evaluation can be needed, particularly when bleeding is new, persistent, recurrent or accompanied by other concerning symptoms.

What treatment options may be discussed?

Depending on symptoms and severity, options may include bowel-habit changes, fibre or fluid advice where appropriate, medicines, office-based procedures or surgery. The right choice depends on an individual assessment.

When should I seek medical attention?

Seek prompt assessment for rectal bleeding, especially if it is persistent or unexplained. Heavy bleeding, black stools, fainting, severe pain or rapidly worsening symptoms require urgent care.

Questions to ask at your appointment

  • What are the likely causes of my symptoms?
  • What examination or tests are appropriate?
  • What options are suitable for my diagnosis and why?
  • What are the risks, alternatives, likely recovery and costs?
  • What symptoms should prompt urgent medical care?

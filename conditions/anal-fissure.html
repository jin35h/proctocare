Anal Fissure: Symptoms, Causes and Care Options

An anal fissure is a small tear in the lining of the anal canal. It may cause sharp pain during or after a bowel movement and sometimes bright-red bleeding.

Important: This guide is general education and cannot diagnose your symptoms. A clinician should assess your individual situation.

Common symptoms

Pain during bowel movements, pain that continues afterwards, and small amounts of bright-red blood may occur. Symptoms can overlap with other anorectal conditions.

Possible contributors

Passing hard stools, constipation, diarrhoea or local trauma may contribute. A clinician can assess the likely cause and whether another condition needs to be considered.

Assessment

Assessment may include symptom history and a careful examination when appropriate. Tell the clinician about symptom duration, medicines, prior episodes and any other health conditions.

Care options

Depending on the case, management may include bowel-habit measures, prescribed topical medication, pain management or a procedure for persistent or complex cases. Discuss benefits, risks and alternatives.

When to seek help

Arrange assessment for persistent pain or bleeding. Seek urgent care for severe or worsening pain, significant bleeding, fever or a painful swelling.

Questions to ask at your appointment

  • What are the likely causes of my symptoms?
  • What examination or tests are appropriate?
  • What options are suitable for my diagnosis and why?
  • What are the risks, alternatives, likely recovery and costs?
  • What symptoms should prompt urgent medical care?

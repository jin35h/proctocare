Anal Fistula: Symptoms, Assessment and Treatment Options

An anal fistula is an abnormal tunnel connecting the anal canal to nearby skin. It can develop after infection or an anorectal abscess and often requires clinical assessment.

Important: This guide is general education and cannot diagnose your symptoms. A clinician should assess your individual situation.

Symptoms that may occur

Symptoms can include recurrent swelling, pain, skin irritation, discharge of pus or blood, and an opening near the anus. Symptoms and severity vary.

Why assessment matters

The fistula’s path and relationship to surrounding muscles affect treatment planning. A clinician may recommend examination and, in selected cases, imaging or other investigations.

Treatment considerations

Treatment may involve a procedure or surgery, but the approach depends on the fistula’s anatomy, complexity and the patient’s health. Ask about healing, recurrence, continence risks, alternatives and follow-up.

Can it be managed at home?

Do not attempt to drain or treat a suspected fistula yourself. Keep the area clean and seek professional advice. Home care cannot reliably close an established fistula.

Urgent symptoms

Fever, increasing pain, spreading redness, a painful swelling or feeling acutely unwell can indicate infection and warrant prompt medical evaluation.

Questions to ask at your appointment

  • What are the likely causes of my symptoms?
  • What examination or tests are appropriate?
  • What options are suitable for my diagnosis and why?
  • What are the risks, alternatives, likely recovery and costs?
  • What symptoms should prompt urgent medical care?

Understand anorectal conditions and your options

Clear, patient-friendly guides to common anorectal concerns. Symptoms can overlap, so diagnosis should be made by a qualified clinician.

◉

Piles (Haemorrhoids)

Swollen vascular tissue around the anus or lower rectum. Bleeding should not automatically be assumed to be piles.

Read guide
◇

Anal fissure

A small tear in the anal lining that may cause pain during bowel movements and bright-red bleeding.

Read guide
⌁

Anal fistula

An abnormal tunnel between the anal canal and nearby skin, often associated with a previous infection or abscess.

Read guide
✳

Pilonidal sinus

A tract or opening in the skin, commonly near the cleft of the buttocks, which may become inflamed or infected.

Read guide
↻

Constipation

Infrequent or difficult bowel movements that may have dietary, medication-related or medical causes.

Read guide
!

Anorectal abscess

A collection of infection that can cause pain, swelling, pus and sometimes fever; urgent evaluation may be needed.

Read guide
Important: Heavy or persistent rectal bleeding, black stools, fainting, severe pain, fever with swelling, or rapidly worsening symptoms require prompt medical evaluation. Seek emergency care when symptoms are severe.
